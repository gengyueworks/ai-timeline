# Public event selection standard

The public edition is a representative historical cut, not a company leaderboard or an application-specific promotional page.

## Selection dimensions

Each event is evaluated on six dimensions:

1. **Technical significance** — did it introduce a lasting capability, architecture, protocol, or research method?
2. **Ecosystem impact** — did developers, researchers, institutions, or users adopt it beyond a single announcement cycle?
3. **Historical distinctiveness** — does it add a new transition rather than repeat another release from the same model family?
4. **Source strength** — is the date and claim supported by a primary or authoritative public source?
5. **Representativeness** — does the public cut cover research, infrastructure, products, safety, standards, and open ecosystems?
6. **Institutional and regional balance** — does the selection avoid allowing one company or country to dominate a period without clear historical justification?

## Same-company rule

For a compact public edition, normally include no more than one closely related model update from the same company in a short period. A second entry requires a distinct historical transition, such as a new product category, protocol, or distribution model.

## What is normally excluded

- routine model refreshes that repeat the same capability story;
- vendor-specific deployment options without broader ecosystem impact;
- weakly sourced trend placeholders;
- events selected to flatter a sponsor, applicant, or preferred company;
- benchmark claims that cannot be independently contextualized.

The 2026 public cut therefore balances OpenAI/Codex, Anthropic, Google, Z.ai, Moonshot, DeepSeek, Meituan, security research, and open protocol development.
