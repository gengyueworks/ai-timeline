# Source and inclusion standard

## Source hierarchy

1. Original papers, official release notes, technical reports, standards, court records, or institutional archives.
2. Reputable independent reporting with named authors and publication dates.
3. High-quality secondary histories when primary material is unavailable.

Social posts, anonymous summaries, generated citations, SEO aggregators, and inaccessible URLs are not sufficient as the sole source for a public event.

## Date policy

Use the date of the event itself—not the date on which a later article discussed it. If only a year or month is known, preserve that uncertainty through `date_precision`. Never invent a day for sorting convenience.

## Confidence

- `high`: directly supported by a primary source or multiple independent authoritative sources.
- `medium`: supported by credible secondary sources, with minor uncertainty.
- `low`: historically plausible but materially disputed; normally excluded from the public dataset until stronger evidence is available.

## Editorial threshold

An event should change the technical direction, capability frontier, infrastructure, developer ecosystem, governance, safety landscape, or public adoption of AI. Product patches and short-lived announcements are excluded unless they represent a broader transition.
